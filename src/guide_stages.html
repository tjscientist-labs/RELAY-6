Thirteen stages. 0–6 are on the bench and prove the electronics; 7–8 put the pad box in its can; 9–11 build the three controllers (any order, any one first); 12 is the first field check. Section 7 (commissioning) follows.

Stage 0 · Order and measure

  1. Order the pad box and core parts from section 5 and one controller's panel parts. Buy two Heltec V3 boards per pair plus one spare.
  2. When the case arrives, measure its inside: length and width at the rim of the base, depth of base and lid, corner radius, and how far the gasket groove stands in. Enter them in src/parts.py and regenerate: every panel outline, hole and DXF is drawn from those numbers.
  3. Measure every CHECK part (key switches, e-stop, arcade buttons, toggles, Powerpole bracket, OLED module) and correct its hole on the cut sheets.
Gate 0. Every CHECK (red) on P-1 to P-9 confirmed or changed; the panel outline fits the case with 1 mm clearance all round.

Stage 1 · Two radios talking

  1. Flash both Heltec boards with a RadioLib "ping-pong" test at 915 MHz (or your region), SF7/125 kHz, CRC on. Antennas fitted before any transmit.
  2. Add the authentication layer: a 16-byte test key, AES-128-CMAC over header and payload, 8-byte tag, 32-bit counter. Log every rejected packet with its reason.
  3. Tamper tests: flip one payload bit (must fail the tag), replay a captured packet (must fail the counter), use a different key (must fail the tag).
Gate 1. 1000 packets each way across the room with none rejected; every tamper test rejected; a power-cycled unit resumes with a higher counter (counter kept in flash).

Stage 2 · Controller core on the bench

  1. On a full-size proto board: Heltec V3 in sockets, 74AHCT125 level shifter, MCP23017 (or the ADS1115 for MONOLITH), headers for the OLED, PN532, MAX98357A and GPS. Wire per E-4.
  2. Check the proposed GPIO map in section 11 against Heltec's V3 pinout before soldering; change the table, not the board, if it disagrees.
  3. Power from the bench supply at 3.7 V, 1 A limit, through the boost converter. Bring up one module at a time: OLED test pattern, encoder counts, PN532 reads a card, a WAV clip plays, GPS gets a fix outdoors, an 8-LED test strip lights.
Gate 2. Every module works together on the one board; current under 400 mA with the LEDs dim; the OLED draws the six screens from the concept sheet with test data.

Stage 3 · Pad box logic and watchdog

  1. Second proto board: Heltec V3, buck converter, U2, U3, U4 and Q7 per E-2. Use a 12 V bench supply at 1 A limit in place of the battery. Fit K1 in its socket but connect nothing to 30, 87 or 87a yet.
  2. Set U2's timing: start with 100 kΩ and 10 µF; toggle HEARTBEAT from a test sketch, then stop. Measure, with a scope or the ESP32's own timer, how long U2's Q stays high after the last edge. Adjust C until it is 0.8–1.2 s and record it in section 11.
  3. Truth table: key OFF / ON × ARM_EN low / high × heartbeat running / stopped. K1 must pull in only for ON + high + running.
  4. Crash test: with K1 pulled in, make the sketch hang in a loop. K1 must drop within the watchdog time.
Gate 3. All eight rows of the truth table correct; K1 drops on a firmware hang; with S1 off K1 cannot pull in.

Stage 4 · Six channels into dummy loads

  1. Build the six channels per E-3 on the power board: the six IRLB3034-type MOSFETs on a common aluminium bar (insulating pads), fuse holders, TVS diodes, gate drivers U5–U7. PAD+ and the return in 12 AWG; each channel in 14 AWG.
  2. Wire K1: 87 to the fused battery feed, 87a to ground, 30 to the PAD+ bus. Check with the meter, power off, that 30 reads 0 Ω to ground.
  3. Six 12 V 21 W lamps on the outputs, bench supply at 12 V, 10 A limit. Arm and fire each pad, then salvo. Each lamp lights for 1.0 s only.
  4. With K1 at rest, command FIRE on every pad from the test sketch: nothing must light (the gate drivers have no supply).
Gate 4. Every pad fires only when armed; no pad lights with K1 at rest whatever the firmware does; MOSFET bar warm at most after a six-lamp salvo.

Stage 5 · Igniter meter calibration

  1. Fit the TEST rail (D0), the six 3.30 kΩ resistors, the input resistors and clamps, and the two ADS1115s.
  2. Put 0.50 Ω, 1.00 Ω and 2.00 Ω (0.1 % or measured on a 4-wire meter) on each pad in turn. Record the readings; store a per-channel offset for lead and contact resistance in flash.
  3. Open circuit reads OPEN; a short reads below 0.2 Ω.
  4. Measure the actual test current with a meter in series: it should be close to 0.9 mA.
Gate 5. All six pads read the reference resistors within ±0.05 Ω; OPEN and SHORT detected.

Stage 6 · Real igniters, no motor

Outdoors, no motor, igniters only. Stand well back as for a launch. Confirm from the igniter's data sheet that the 0.9 mA test current is far below its no-fire current before connecting it. If it is not, reduce it (raise the 3.30 kΩ resistors) and repeat Stage 5.
  1. Battery in place of the bench supply, F0 fitted. Igniters on all six pads.
  2. Fire each mode from the controller. Check the FIRED report gives a time for each pad.
  3. Fire a three-igniter cluster on one pad. Read the current from the INA228 log; it must stay under the 15 A pad fuse.
Gate 6. Every igniter lit; every ignition time reported; no fuse blown; battery voltage recovers after a salvo; the INA228 log shows the salvo peak current, and the pad box stayed powered through it.

Stage 7 · The pad box can

  1. Tape the 1:1 templates P-8 (front) and P-9 (lid) to the can and centre-punch: six Powerpole outputs and the key on the front, the mast socket, antenna bulkhead and anemometer post on the lid, charge port and main switch on the side.
  2. Drill and punch, deburr, then paint the inside of every hole to stop rust. Fit grommets and the PG7 gland.
  3. Fit an inner plate (6 mm plywood or HDPE) carrying the battery strap, the power board and the logic board. The battery sits low and is strapped; nothing rests on it.
{V3} {P8}
Gate 7. The lid closes over the wiring with the gasket seated; every hole is deburred and sealed; the board plate is fixed at four points.

Stage 8 · Pad box assembly

  1. Move the boards into the can. Re-run Gates 3, 4 and 5 in the can.
  2. Fit the beacon mast (two 12 in lengths of ½ in aluminium tube joined by a short internal coupler, plugged into the lid socket) and the anemometer. Check the WIND reading against a hand anemometer or a weather report.
  3. Label every output 1–6, red +, black −, and the key positions SAFE / ARM.
Gate 8. Gates 3–5 pass again with the lid closed; beacon visible from 50 m in daylight.

Stage 9 · HALO

  1. Cut the HALO panel (P-1, 3 mm black acrylic), the lid insert (P-2) and the ring, flip cover and hinge blocks (P-3: 6 mm clear, 3 mm clear, 6 mm black). Sand the ring's bottom face so it diffuses.
  2. Glue the WS2812 strip in a ring under the four arc slots, LEDs facing up; bond the clear ring to the four bridges on top with acrylic cement. Fit the six 30 mm buttons with a NeoPixel mini PCB in each, the 60 mm FIRE button, the cover microswitch, key switch, e-stop, encoder and NFC window.
  3. Panel on four standoffs from the case wall cleats; core board under it. Lid: OLED in its bezel, speaker, antenna bulkhead in the case wall.
{V_HALO} {P1}
Gate 9. Ring lights evenly with no hot spots; FIRE does nothing with the cover closed; full Stage 4 lamp test from the HALO.

Stage 10 · FLIGHTDECK

  1. Order the aluminium panel from P-4 (laser cut, powder coated) or cut 3 mm acrylic for a first version; the lid insert is P-5.
  2. Fit the six covered toggles, the six NeoPixel Stick-8 status bars behind a frosted strip, the ARM and FIRE buttons as far apart as the panel allows (252 mm centres), the master key, e-stop and mode encoder.
  3. ARM and FIRE are wired in series into the one FIRE input: either released, the input opens. Lid: OLED and the checklist card over an edge-lit acrylic sheet.
{V_FD} {P4}
Gate 10. Neither button alone starts a count; a lifted cover shows its pad in the selection; full lamp test.

Stage 11 · MONOLITH

  1. Cut the panel from P-6 as two layers of 3 mm black acrylic and laminate them (or 6.9 mm acetal from a CNC service); lid insert P-7. Confirm the slide-pot lever width before cutting the slots.
  2. Fit the six slide potentiometers under the slots with machined or printed lever caps; one WS2812 strip under each slot. "Committed" is the top 10 % of travel.
  3. Fire core: the 30 mm illuminated button sits in the 6810 bearing's bore; a printed collar on the bearing carries a cam that closes the COVER microswitch at 90°, and a torsion spring returns it. Twin OLEDs in the lid share SPI with separate chip selects.
{V_MONO} {P6}
Gate 11. Collar returns to SAFE on its own from any angle; levers read cleanly end to end; full lamp test.
